Next Up

Privacy Policy

The short version: Next Up collects no data. There's no account, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and the app contains no third-party code. What you set up in Next Up stays on your iPhone.

What stays on your iPhone

  • Your alarms, the shows you follow, the episodes you download, where you left off in each, and your settings.
  • Your approximate location, only if you choose My Approximate Location (Explore › Look and Feel › Location): it places the sun, moon, and stars in Next Up's sky. It's rounded to about 11 km, only the last one is kept, and it's removed when you choose something else. Otherwise the sky follows your time zone, or a place you pick from the list.
  • A wake log, a record of what each alarm did, kept on your iPhone to diagnose problems. It leaves your iPhone only if you choose to attach it when you report an issue; then it's used only to answer you, and deleted when you ask.
  • iOS includes Next Up's alarms and settings in your iPhone's own backup (to iCloud or your computer, under your Apple Account). Downloaded episodes are left out of backups.

Next Up has no server of its own, so none of this can be seen or recovered by anyone but you, unless you send it.

What leaves your iPhone, and where it goes

Next Up connects only to the places it needs to do what you ask:

  • Apple. When you search for a show, paste an Apple Podcasts link, or bring shows over from an earlier version of Next Up or from a Shortcut or a link, Next Up asks Apple's podcast directory, by a show's name or its Apple Podcasts number; the artwork of search results and of the recommended shows (a list built into the app) comes from Apple's servers. When you buy or restore Next Up Unlimited, the App Store handles it: Next Up learns only whether the unlock is yours, never your payment details. Apple's privacy policy covers these.
  • Podcast publishers. Each show's feed, artwork, episodes, chapters and transcripts come straight from its publisher's servers (or the hosting service it uses), as in any podcast app. Those servers see the request, including your IP address and, as with any app, which app is asking and your iOS version. What a publisher does with its own server records is covered by its own policy.

Nothing else is sent: no identifiers, no usage statistics, and no reports of your alarms or of what you listen to, unless you send us a report (Explore › Get in Touch › Report a Problem), with the wake log if you choose to attach it.

Email

If you write to support@nextupalarm.com, or use Report a Problem in Next Up, your message and your address are used only to answer you, and deleted whenever you ask. A report from Next Up includes the app's version, the iOS version and your iPhone's model, and the wake log if you choose.

Permissions

Next Up asks for two: alarms, so an alarm goes off even when Next Up is closed, and notifications, to show the morning's podcast with Snooze and Stop on the Lock Screen, and the Nightstand reminder if you turn it on. Both work entirely on your iPhone, and you can change either in the Settings app. A third only if you choose My Approximate Location: your approximate location, used on your iPhone only to place the sun, moon, and stars in Next Up's sky, and never sent.

Children

Next Up collects nothing from anyone, children included.

Earlier versions

Versions of Next Up before 3.0 included Facebook's software development kit, which counted app events. Next Up 3.0 removed it.

Changes and questions

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with its date. Questions are welcome at support@nextupalarm.com.

This policy applies to Next Up 3.0 and later, and is effective as of November 1, 2026.
